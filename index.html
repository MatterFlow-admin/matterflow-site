01Mission

Our mission

MatterFlow builds alongside the people who keep live events running, from operations leads to the crews on the line. We sort what can be recovered, audit everything that passes through, and let it flow back into the systems venues already run. We start with aluminum, because crews already pull it first and it can be recycled indefinitely. By bridging the gap between people, technology, and sustainability, we make recovery the logical choice for every venue and the crews and operations teams who keep it running.

02The problem

Every crowd leaves a mountain behind it.

Bags arrive at the dock faster than anyone can work through them. A crew sorts by hand, against the clock, in a room that was never designed to grow. How much gets recovered depends on how long the shift can hold out.

With the 2028 Games coming to Los Angeles, venue waste volumes and scrutiny are about to grow.

  1. 01

    Every live event produces a large, predictable volume of mixed waste in a short window.

  2. 02

    Recovering material takes labor, time, and space, which venues have least of after an event.

  3. 03

    When sorting costs more than the team has in that moment, mixed waste goes to landfill. That is a rational decision, not a lack of care.

  4. 04

    MatterFlow lowers that cost by automating the first and most repetitive pick, aluminum, and adds value by auditing everything that passes through.

  5. 05

    Once value exceeds cost, recovery becomes the logical choice.

03How it works

Identify. Kick.
Repeat.

A gravity chute with on-device computer vision and a paddle. It sits in the back-of-house area a venue already has and takes the first, most repetitive pick off the crew. Everything else keeps moving through the line they already run.

  1. 1

    Load.

    Bags come off the floor as collected. The crew feeds material into the top of the chute. Nothing is asked of fans.

  2. 2

    Identify.

    Items slide down the chute past a camera. An on-device model classifies each one in motion.

  3. 3

    Kick.

    A paddle diverts aluminum into its own collection bin.

  4. 4

    Flow.

    Everything else continues to the existing line for the crew.

  5. 5

    Log.

    Every decision is recorded and becomes the venue's audit record.

04Waste auditing

Your Data.
In Your Hands.

Auditing is as central as sorting. Every item that passes the camera is logged, so each event ends with a record of what came in and where it went, not just a hauling bill.

What venues use it for

A

TRUE and LEED reporting

Item-level records for certification reporting, gathered as the sort runs instead of estimated afterward.

B

ESG and sustainability reports

Per-event diversion and energy figures in a form the venue can drop into its own reports.

C

Operations planning

What comes in, and when, to plan staffing, hauling, and bin placement for the next event.

D

Proof of impact

A defensible number as public scrutiny of venue waste grows.

  • Jointly owned by the venue and MatterFlow
  • Processed on the edge
  • Cameras point at waste, not people
  • No personal data
  • Included in the service, no separate fee

05What changes

What changes

Space.

About twenty-four square feet inside the back-of-house area a venue already has. Not a new sorting floor.

Time.

The first pass runs at machine speed instead of hand speed, so the dock clears sooner.

Labor.

Crews stay on the line with less repetitive picking. Their time moves to the calls that need judgment.

1day

Target: take a full day out of the post-event sort.

This is the goal the system is designed against. It is a target, not a result. The Coliseum pilot is where it gets measured.

06Pilot venue

Built with the venue,
not for it.

MatterFlow exists because of what we found at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum. We spent our discovery there, in back of house during load-out, working alongside the crews who move the waste. The product is shaped by what that showed us.

The Coliseum is our target pilot venue, and the system is being developed with its operations team. One venue, in depth, before anything else.

07Where we're going

The machine is
the vehicle.

In five to ten years, we want MatterFlow to be the standard back-of-house waste layer for venues of every size, from fully resourced arenas to bare-bones operations. Around the machine sits an ecosystem. A venue can start with one piece and grow into the rest.

  1. 01

    Sorting service

    The unit on the line, starting with aluminum and adding streams as each one pays for itself.

  2. 02

    Vision model

    The computer vision model behind the sort, licensable on its own.

  3. 03

    Waste data auditing

    Item-level auditing as a standalone service.

  4. 04

    Data products

    Every event turned into a measurable record.

The goal is to shift how operators think about waste: from a cost they haul away to a process they can measure and improve.
